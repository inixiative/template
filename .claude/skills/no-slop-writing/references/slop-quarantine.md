# Slop Quarantine

Patterns that mark writing as generic-AI or vendor-marketing rather than advisory. Reject on sight while drafting, not just at review. Each entry gives the tell and, where useful, the genre-correct rewrite.

---

## The teaching case: one document, two registers

Anchor 1 in `register-anchors.md` (the clean CDP recommendation) and the passages below both come from **the same consulting deck**. The genre is not a property of the document or the author. It is a property of discipline. The moment the writing stops carrying facts and starts selling, it leaves the genre, even inside a genuine consulting deliverable.

**Vendor-marketing slop (from the same deck's tool write-ups):**

> "The Treasure Data Journey Orchestration platform features an intuitive drag-and-drop interface, enabling users to craft customer journeys seamlessly integrated with every stage of the purchasing process..."

> "[Segment] empowers businesses to make informed, data-driven decisions and deliver personalized experiences to their customers across channels... to maximize relevance and engagement... to optimize outcomes."

> "The synergistic capabilities of LiveRamp and Habu's fully interoperable clean room technology enable seamless collaboration... amplifying the spectrum of achievable use cases and outcomes."

**Tells:** empower, seamless / seamlessly, synergistic, cutting-edge, pioneering, intuitive, amplifying the spectrum, data-driven (as decoration), "deliver personalized experiences," verb-stacking ("craft... integrate... maximize... optimize"). None of it carries a number, a threshold, a named constraint, or a decision. It could describe any tool.

**Genre-correct rewrite (what the advisory register would say instead):**

> "Treasure Data provides journey orchestration and no-code segment building. It lacks native clean-room matching, so retail-media use cases would require pairing it with LiveRamp or Habu. Best fit if the priority is loyalty orchestration over data collaboration."

The rewrite names what the tool does, what it does not do, and the decision that follows. That is the test.

## Agency rule: people make the decisions (check this first)

This is the most common generic-AI tell in analytical prose, and the one readers feel before they can name it. Three moves usually travel together:

1. **An actorless past participle sets up the subject.** "A tool bought for one category...", "Budgets allocated last year...", "Data collected across channels...". A decision happened, and the sentence hides who made it.
2. **The tense then jumps to present and the object takes the active verb.** "...keeps adding capabilities," "it surfaces at renewal," "the overlap creeps in," "nobody owns it." The thing now acts. The people are gone.
3. **The result reads like weather.** Events happen around marketers who appear unable to touch them. That is usually false: people own these decisions, and the reader is often the person who can change the outcome.

**Rule.** Stay in third person, and give every decision a human owner. Name who acted (marketers, the team, procurement, the agency, the vendor). Keep tense consistent: past decisions in past tense with a named actor, current conditions in present tense, and no mid-sentence slide from one to the other.

**When an object can be the subject.** When the verb describes what the thing mechanically does, or what a vendor does to its own product: "Vendors expand their products." "The platform stores first-party data." An object cannot be the subject when the verb stands in for a human decision, judgment or consequence. Watch for: surfaces, emerges, creeps in, falls through the cracks, keeps adding, ends up as, becomes a conversation, drives (a decision), nobody owns, nobody chose.

**Word choice.** "Bought" frames adoption as a transaction. When describing why a team chose a tool, use adopted, selected or brought in.

**Test.** For each sentence, ask "who decided this?" If the answer is a person or team and the sentence does not name them, rewrite it. If the sentence says nobody owns something the reader in fact owns, rewrite it.

**Before (agentless, tense drift):**

> "A tool bought for one category keeps adding capabilities that belong to another. Nobody chose the duplicate, so nobody owns it, and it surfaces at renewal as a price conversation when it could have been a capability conversation."

**After (people in control, consistent tense):**

> "Teams adopt a tool for a specific set of capabilities. As vendors expand their products, those capabilities start to overlap with other tools in the stack, and each vendor wants to charge for the new features. Marketers who track that overlap as it develops can decide at renewal which tool keeps each capability, and set price from that decision."

## Prohibited constructions (these are the genre's rules, applied across every deliverable)

**Em dashes.** Never, in any function. Use a period, comma, or colon.

**"It's not X. It's Y" reframes.** Prohibited unless X is something the reader genuinely believed and Y is a non-obvious correction. If the gap is modest, cut the construction and state Y.

**Rhetorical questions as transitions.** "So what does this mean?" / "But here's the real question:" are banned. Earn the transition structurally or don't transition.

**Emphasis fragments.** "That matters." / "The stakes are real." / "This is the shift." If the short sentence isn't the sharpest version of the idea, cut it.

**Colons that hype.** Colons deliver information (see Anchor 5). They do not build suspense.

**Stacked synonym adjectives.** Pick the most accurate one. "robust, scalable, powerful platform" → name the one property that matters.

**Affirmation openers.** No "Absolutely," "Certainly," "Great question." Start with the substance.

**"It's worth noting that" / "It's important to understand that."** Cut. State the thing.

**Truth-intensifiers.** "actually," "genuinely," "really," "truly," "in reality," "the reality is." They fake contrarian depth. Never in a heading or subject line.

**Tacked-on reflective codas.** ", which is why...", ", which is where...", ", which is what...". If the summary earns a sentence, write one. Otherwise cut.

**Default rule-of-three.** Don't pad to three reasons, signals, or examples for rhythm. Two real points → two. Five → five. Let the count follow the content.

**Insight-cosplay metaphors.** "load-bearing," "says the quiet part out loud," "where it lands," "the move," "the through-line," "the tell," "at its core," "table stakes," "the sharpest version of," "the honest answer."

**Corporate-polish vocabulary.** leverage, robust, seamless, navigate (figurative), underscore, foster, empower, unlock, elevate, streamline, delve, dive into, ecosystem, landscape ("the X landscape"), realm, tapestry, nuanced, crucial, pivotal, vital, compelling, myriad, plethora, game-changer, cutting-edge, best-in-class, holistic, synergistic, pioneering. Use the plain word.

**Filler transition openers.** "Here's the thing," "That said," "At the end of the day," "In today's landscape," "Make no mistake," "Simply put."

## Positive discipline (the flip side)

Write paragraphs that breathe unevenly; vary length; let some end before the idea is fully wrapped. Show hierarchy: the most important item in a list should feel most important, not merely appear first. Match register to the weight of the content; do not inflate an ordinary observation with the cadence of insight, and do not underplay a genuinely sharp finding with flat delivery. It is fine to say you don't know rather than fill the gap.

## Self-check before delivery

1. Does every sentence carry a fact from the table, or connect two that do? If not, cut it.
2. Ask "who decided this?" of every sentence that describes a choice or consequence. Each one names a human owner, and no object takes an active verb that belongs to a person. Check that tense holds steady within each sentence.
3. Scan for any word in the corporate-polish list and any construction above. Zero tolerance.
4. Would this sentence fit inside one of the register anchors? If not, it is out of genre.
5. Read the opening line. If it is throat-clearing, delete it and start at the claim.
