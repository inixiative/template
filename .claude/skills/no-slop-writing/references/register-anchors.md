# Register Anchors

Verbatim, slop-free specimens of the advisory genre, drawn from real consulting deliverables. **Authorship is irrelevant.** The target is the genre, not the writer. They come from consulting readouts written by other firms, with client names removed. What matters is that each passage is a clean instance of the genre and shows exactly one thing to imitate.

Before drafting, pick the anchor matching your deliverable type and continue in that register as if extending the same document.

---

## Anchor 1 — Advisory recommendation prose (memo / proposal)

> "We recommend that the retailer would be best served in the mid to long term (across Loyalty, Marketing and Retail Media) with a full-service or customisable CDP subject to vendor flexibility commitments. As a first step, we recommend you run tests using your existing pipes to prove the business case ahead of buying in a CDP. We recommend you then run an RFP with a list of the CDP providers we have suggested, and we structure the RFP to ask vendors to propose how they would integrate with Salesforce and the benefits of using their decisioning engine capability vs a stand-alone decisioning engine. We have structured the same RFP previously so can support you."

**Why it is clean:** Every sentence carries a decision or a next action. The recommendation is stated once, then sequenced ("As a first step... We recommend you then..."). The one hedge is concrete and load-bearing ("subject to vendor flexibility commitments"), not a vague softener. No adjective works to impress. It closes on a specific offer of help, not a flourish.

**Imitate:** Recommendation → sequence → concrete condition → what we can do about it. This is the spine of a proposal or memo of advice.

## Anchor 2 — Executive-summary headline (quantified, itemized)

> "The revenue opportunity will deliver £75m incremental revenue on the required £1.2–1.7m investment (£780k tech, £500k–£1m people). Throughout the discovery phase we have established that the lowest-hanging fruit to drive incremental revenue growth is across retail media, loyalty programme, and increased addressability to reach new audiences."

**Why it is clean:** The headline is the number and the trade (£75m on £1.2–1.7m), broken into its parts in the same breath. The claim of provenance is plain ("Throughout the discovery phase we have established"), not dramatized.

**Imitate:** Lead an executive summary with the quantified claim and its components. Never open with scene-setting before the number.

## Anchor 3 — Analytical deck: action-title + evidence (slide register)

> **Title:** "The brand's TV spend is above wear-out limits, suggesting limited impact from reducing TV"
> **Body:** "Above 3,000 GRPs, ads begin to lose messaging effectiveness. At 500 GRPs, ads begin to drive aided brand awareness. The brand appears to purchase more premium inventory, leading to lower GRPs at higher costs."

**Why it is clean:** The title is a full-sentence finding that carries its own so-what ("suggesting limited impact from reducing TV"). The body is thresholds and a mechanism, not prose. Nothing on the slide exists to transition or to impress.

**Imitate:** For decks only. The title states the finding and its implication; every supporting line carries a number, a threshold, or a named mechanism. Do not write slide bodies as paragraphs.

---

## Anchor 4 — "What needs to be true" reasoning (falsifiable framing)

> "What we need to believe to increase digital as part of the mix: there are more pet parents available in digital channels that we can reach effectively at the right level of frequency; digital is efficient and effective for broad targeting outside of strategic audiences; there is opportunity to reduce TV spend, hence reduce total media spend, increasing digital as part of the mix."

**Why it is clean:** The recommendation is put as a set of conditions that could be proven false, each stated flatly. It does not assert the conclusion; it exposes the load the conclusion has to bear. This is how the genre handles a contestable recommendation honestly.

**Imitate:** When a recommendation depends on assumptions, frame it as "what has to be true for this to hold," each condition falsifiable and specific. Reach for this whenever you would otherwise overclaim.

---

## Anchor 5 — Balanced diagnostic finding (claim + substantiation)

> "The fundamental CRM data the retailer have in place is complete: there are no gaps in what we expect to see in a loyalty programme data set. The marketing team push through a high volume of work and step out of their comfort zones to keep the business moving forward, despite not having all the systems and tools required to do their jobs as efficiently as they would like."

**Why it is clean:** The colon delivers information ("complete: there are no gaps..."), it does not manufacture suspense. Findings are stated with their specific substantiation. A diagnostic that only lists problems reads as a pitch; this one credits what works, which is what makes the criticism land.

**Imitate:** State the finding, then the evidence, plainly. In any diagnostic, name what is working before and alongside what is not. Use colons to deliver, never to hype.

---

## How to use these

- Match the anchor to the deliverable, then write as if you are continuing that exact document.
- If your draft sentence would not fit inside the anchor passage, it does not belong in the deliverable.
- When two anchors could apply (e.g. an executive summary inside a proposal), the more specific deliverable row in the SKILL selector governs the framing; the anchors govern the sentence-level register.
